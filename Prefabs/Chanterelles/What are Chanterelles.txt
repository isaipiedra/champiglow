Chanterelle is a name used for several species of mushrooms found in the genera Cantharellus, Craterellus, Gomphus and Polyozellus. They are yellow, and have the “folds” running down the stipe. These folds are not true gills, and are thus a distinguishing feature from their common lookalike False Chanterelles. These mushrooms are a culinary delicacy, and are sought after by mushroom hunters all over. These mushrooms are well preserved when dried.

These mushrooms form a mycorrhizal symbiosis with living trees, where the fungal network (mycelium) intertwines with tree roots. These mushrooms require this symbiotic relationship with tree roots to survive, making it very difficult to cultivate these mushrooms. These mushrooms are commonly found on the forest floor next to trees, occasionally growing in fairy rings. Chanterelles are associated with either conifers or hardwood trees, depending on species. They are often found with several soft and hardwoods like oaks, birch, pine, beech, chestnut, and western hemlock. Common lookalikes are the False Chanterelle and Jack O’ Lanterns, which are darker orange than Chanterelles.

Never consume mushrooms you aren’t 100% sure of! This quick writeup is meant to be informative. If you wish to find and consume mushrooms from the wild, ensure you have the correct mushroom. I am not responsible if you attempt to eat a mushroom that looks like a mushroom from this pack, as there can be potentially many different poisonous lookalikes. If you wish to do your own mushroom hunting, check out some mushroom guide books local to your area (ie A Field Guide To Edible Mushrooms Of The Pacific Northwest if you live in the PNW). You can also try to find local guides who do mushroom hunts where you can learn more about mushrooms in your area.
