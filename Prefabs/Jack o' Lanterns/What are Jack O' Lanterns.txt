Omphalotus olearius, also known as the Jack O’ Lantern mushroom. Aptly named because it is orange, and glows in the dark. These spectacular mushrooms are unfortunately poisonous, and are considered a common lookalike to avoid for Chanterelles. If consumed, they are not typically lethal, but will cause severe cramps, vomiting, and diarrhea. These mushrooms glow blue-green during low light, and after the eyes have adjusted, meaning the glow is actually fairly faint, despite what they can look like in photos.

These mushrooms can be found commonly where Chanterelle’s grow. These mushrooms form a mycorrhizal symbiosis with living trees, where the fungal network (mycelium) intertwines with tree roots. These mushrooms require this symbiotic relationship with tree roots to survive, making it very difficult to cultivate these mushrooms. These mushrooms are commonly found on the forest floor next to deciduous or hardwood trees. They sometimes are considered a lookalike to Chicken of the Woods, due to having similar colors.
