Laetiporus sp., These mushrooms are known as Chicken of the Woods due to the fact that when cooked, it feels and tastes like chicken. This mushroom comes in a brilliant orange that is commonly mistaken with the Jack O’ Lantern mushrooms. Like most other choice conks that grow on wood, this mushroom is a Polypore. This means that it has pores instead of gills, much like Boletes. This Polypore is a choice edible amongst mushroom hunters and tasters alike. It is a medicinal mushroom with potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties. It contains bioactive compounds like eburicoric acid and quercetin, which may help fight tumors, boost the immune system, and reduce inflammation.

Chicken of the Woods tends to grow on open decaying wounded trees. This mushroom is parasitic, meaning it grows on living trees, taking nutrients from them. If the rot from this mushroom spreads to the heart of the tree, it will cause the tree to fall over and/or die. This means this mushroom can be devastating to any living trees it grows on. Even if you pick the fruiting bodies (ie the mushrooms), the mycelium is still spreading through the tree. Once a tree is infected with this mushroom, it is rotting, and most likely won’t survive having this mushroom infecting it. Common lookalikes are Jack O’ Lantern mushrooms, which are distinguished by the fact they have gills instead of pores, and the fact they don’t grow parasitically on trees.

Never consume mushrooms you aren’t 100% sure of! This quick writeup is meant to be informative. If you wish to find and consume mushrooms from the wild, ensure you have the correct mushroom. I am not responsible if you attempt to eat a mushroom that looks like a mushroom from this pack, as there can be potentially many different poisonous lookalikes. If you wish to do your own mushroom hunting, check out some mushroom guide books local to your area (ie A Field Guide To Edible Mushrooms Of The Pacific Northwest if you live in the PNW). You can also try to find local guides who do mushroom hunts where you can learn more about mushrooms in your area.
