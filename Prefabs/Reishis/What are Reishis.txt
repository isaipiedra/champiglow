Ganoderma sichuanense, also known as Reishi or Lingzhi mushroom (霊芝) is a revered medicinal mushroom originating from ancient China. Like most other choice conks that grow on wood, this mushroom is a Polypore. This means that it has pores instead of gills, much like Boletes. This Polypore mushroom was historically known as the "mushroom of immortality", and has been used by people for over 2000 years. This mushroom is edible, but the taste is fairly bitter, and the flesh is very woody. In order to ingest this mushroom, it was originally prepared as a tea. Nowadays, it is very common in mushroom supplements. This mushroom is commonly used as an adaptogen to boost the immune system, reduce stress, improve sleep, and manage fatigue. It contains bioactive compounds like triterpenoids and beta-glucans that offer anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and potential anti-cancer benefits. 

Reishi mushrooms are both saprobic and parasitic, depending on the tree they’re growing on. This means that if they’re growing on a living tree, then they are parasitic. If they’re growing on a dead or decaying tree, they are saprobic because they are decomposing the tree. Reishi tends to grow on decaying hardwood trees and stumps, particularly oaks, maples, and birch, as well as on coniferous trees like hemlock. Common lookalikes are other Ganoderma species, and Red-Belted Polypore (Fomitopsis pinicola), which is commonly found on pine trees.
