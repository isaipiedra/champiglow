Pleurotus ostreatus, commonly known as Oyster Mushrooms, is a prized edible and medicinal mushroom found and cultivated throughout the world. The species has been found to contain the cholesterol-lowering drug lovastatin. Named Oyster mushrooms due to the fact the cap looks like the Oyster shellfish. This mushroom is very resilient, and has been known to break down very complex organic materials, oils, pollutants, and even some plastics.  This makes it one of the best bio-degraders in the world. Oyster mushrooms have been used to treat soil that had been polluted with diesel oil. The mushroom was able to convert 95% of the oil into non-toxic compounds. One of the coolest mushrooms around, despite being somewhat common.

These mushrooms are commonly growing in clusters found on dying or dead hardwood/deciduous trees. Though they can be found on living trees, they are not parasitic. They are growing on the tree because the tree is dying, and these mushrooms are decomposing it. These mushrooms are very beneficial to the forest ecosystem because they decompose dead trees quickly so they can return back to the soil and revitalize it further. They are most commonly found on dead and decaying trees.
