Hericium erinaceus, more commonly known as Lions Mane mushroom. This mushroom looks funny because it has dangling spines instead of gills for producing spores. Despite its funny looks, it is a highly prized edible mushroom with medicinal properties. Ancient Chinese medical practitioners used this mushroom to heighten brain function and memory. This mushroom can be easily cultivated indoors as well. Due to its light and fluffy texture, and its thin splines, this mushroom is really good at absorbing sauces and flavors when cooked. This mushroom is commonly found in mushroom supplements.

These mushrooms are commonly growing in clusters found on dying or dead hardwood/deciduous trees. Most commonly on maple, ash, oaks, and eucalyptus. Though they can be found on living trees, they are not parasitic. They are growing on the tree because the tree is dying, and these mushrooms are decomposing it. These mushrooms are very beneficial to the forest ecosystem because they decompose dead trees quickly so they can return back to the soil and revitalize it further. They are most commonly found on dead and decaying trees.
