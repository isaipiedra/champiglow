Lentinula edodes, also known as Shiitake, is a mushroom native to east Asia regions. The mushroom's Japanese name shiitake (椎茸) is a compound word composed of shii (椎), for the tree Japanese tsuburajii, (円椎) that provides the dead logs on which it is typically cultivated, and take (茸; "mushroom"). Nowadays, Shiitake mushrooms are cultivated and eaten around the world. This mushroom has been potentially cultivated for close to 800 years ago in ancient China. Research into  Shiitake has shown mushrooms may help lower high blood pressure and improve heart health due to compounds like eritadenine, sterols, and beta-glucans that relax blood vessels and reduce cholesterol. 

Shiitake grow in groups on the decaying wood of deciduous trees, particularly shii and other chinquapins, chestnut, oak, maple, beech, sweetgum, poplar, hornbeam, ironwood, and mulberry. Its natural distribution includes warm and moist climates in Southeast Asia. The most critical lookalike is the deadly Galerina marginata, which shares a similar woody habitat. Reliable identification relies on white spore prints (not brown), a tough, fibrous stem, and a lack of a ring on the stem.
