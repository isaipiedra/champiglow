Trametes versicolor, also known as Turkey Tail mushrooms. These mushrooms are aptly named due to the fact they look like Turkey's Tails. These mushrooms can grow in a brilliant prismatic look. I wish I could have done better on showing these guys on my pack, but hopefully you like what I made. Like most other choice conks that grow on wood, this mushroom is a Polypore. This means that it has pores instead of gills, much like Boletes. This polypore is edible and is a mushroom of choice amongst mushroom hunters and tasters alike. Turkey Tails are saprophytic, meaning they mostly grow on dead or decaying wood. Turkey Tails are a medicinal mushroom rich in antioxidants and polysaccharides (specifically PSK and PSP) that boost immune function and support cancer treatments, particularly by reducing chemotherapy side effects. 

These mushrooms are found worldwide in wooded areas. These mushrooms are commonly found growing on dead or dying hardwood trees like Oak, Beech, Birch, Maple, Alder, Ash, Aspen, Chestnut, Elm, Hickory, Plum, Poplar, and Sweet Gum trees. The most common lookalike to look out for is the False Turkey Tail (Stereum ostrea), which is inedible.
