Morchella sp., commonly known as Morel mushrooms, are an extremely prized edible and medicinal mushroom, sought after by mushroom enthusiasts all over the world. This mushroom is one of the best tasting mushrooms around. These mushrooms don’t have gills, and instead have pits that release their spores. The ridges are sterile and don’t release any spores. This makes it somewhat difficult to obtain a spore print. These funny looking mushrooms are well preserved when dried. Morels were traditionally used in ancient Chinese medicine, and are currently being researched for treating cancer, inflammation, and potential uses for their anti-oxidants.

These mushrooms form a mycorrhizal symbiosis with living trees, where the fungal network (mycelium) intertwines with tree roots. These mushrooms require this symbiotic relationship with tree roots to survive, making it very difficult to cultivate these mushrooms. These mushrooms are commonly found growing individually or in clusters on the forest floor next to trees. These mushrooms can be found near both softwood and hardwood trees. Common lookalike is the False Morel. To distinguish it, True Morels have a hollow stem, and the stem connects at the bottom of the cap. False Morels are not hollow and the top connects to the top of the cap when split open.

Never consume mushrooms you aren’t 100% sure of! This quick writeup is meant to be informative. If you wish to find and consume mushrooms from the wild, ensure you have the correct mushroom. I am not responsible if you attempt to eat a mushroom that looks like a mushroom from this pack, as there can be potentially many different poisonous lookalikes. If you wish to do your own mushroom hunting, check out some mushroom guide books local to your area (ie A Field Guide To Edible Mushrooms Of The Pacific Northwest if you live in the PNW). You can also try to find local guides who do mushroom hunts where you can learn more about mushrooms in your area.
