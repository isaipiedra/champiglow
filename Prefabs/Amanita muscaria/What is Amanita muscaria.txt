Amanita muscaria, also known as Fly Agaric mushrooms, due to the fact these have traditionally been used as an insect repellent. Bright red and white spots, these iconic mushrooms are found in the Super Mario games. Some speculate that Mario increasing in size may have been a pop culture reference to the hallucinations these mushrooms can give. If these mushrooms are ingested raw, they can cause severe gastronomical pain and hallucinations, and are thus considered poisonous. Some cultures will eat these mushrooms by cooking them first to try to convert the Ibotenic Acid into Muscimol, to try to mitigate the nausea and stomach pain, while still keeping the dissociative hallucinations. The hallucinations these can give can be quite scary, and thus are not recommended by any mushroom hunters to ever consume. Some say that vikings in the past used these mushrooms to enter a “berserker” state of mind, and could increase their battle prowess. 

These mushrooms are found throughout the northern Boreal and temperate forests of the world. These mushrooms form a mycorrhizal symbiosis with living trees, where the fungal network (mycelium) intertwines with tree roots. These mushrooms require this symbiotic relationship with tree roots to survive, making it very difficult to cultivate these mushrooms. These mushrooms are commonly found on the forest floor next to trees, occasionally growing in fairy rings. Commonly found with soft and hardwoods like pine, oak, spruce, fir, birch, and cedar. Common lookalikes for these are other species of the Amanita family like A. caesarea. Immature specimens can be mistaken for the Death Cap mushroom (Amanita phalloides), which is one of the deadliest mushrooms in the world.
